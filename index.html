Signal Detection

Live news-volume anomaly detection via GDELT — a real z-score computed against each keyword's 60-day baseline, refreshed automatically every 12 hours. A leading indicator layer, separate from the confirmed WHO/CDC feed.

⚠ Unverified signals, not confirmed outbreaks. These are statistical anomalies in news-mention volume — they can be driven by a single viral article, an unrelated event sharing keywords, or genuine early outbreak chatter. Cross-check against the Outbreak Feed and primary sources before treating any of this as actionable. A spike is a prompt to look closer, not a finding.

Fetched automatically by a scheduled GitHub Actions job — not by your browser, which is why this works reliably regardless of your network. Browser-side calls to GDELT were persistently blocked across multiple tests; running the same fetch server-side, correctly paced (up to ~45s between requests, with retries on rate-limit/timeout responses), resolved it.

Historical Dual-Use Incidents Map

Three distinct categories of "flagged" — kept separate deliberately. Category A is research or material formally flagged by oversight/export-control bodies (no attack occurred). Category B is confirmed deliberate use of a biological agent to cause harm. Category C is an accidental biosafety failure — no intent to harm, but a real containment or material-control lapse. Sourced from FBI/DOJ records, BIS enforcement actions, peer-reviewed journals, GovAI's "A Biosafety Agenda for AI-Bio Governance" (Sudarshan & Hersman, Aug 2026), and the Global Terrorism Database (START/University of Maryland, Kaggle mirror through 2017).

Category A — flagged, no attack Category B — confirmed use of agent Category C — accidental biosafety failure

Excluded deliberately: ~15 GTD-logged 2001–2011 hoax "white powder" letters (US, Kenya, Pakistan, Chile) riding the Amerithrax panic — real GTD entries, but near-identical, zero-casualty, and would clutter the map without adding distinct value. Also excluded: a GTD entry for a 23 May 2000 Salmonella poisoning of Israeli tourists (2 deaths) — GTD is the only source found for this and it could not be independently corroborated, so it is omitted rather than presented as confirmed. Also excluded: a 1999 Oblivskaya CCHF outbreak allegedly linked to a former Soviet bioweapon facility — the GovAI report that surfaced it states attribution and mechanism are uncertain, so it stays out.

Candidate future source, not yet incorporated: "Determinants of Fatalities and Secondary Transmission in Laboratory Pathogen Incidents, 1900–2025" (ACHS, Journal of Infection) — a long-run dataset on laboratory pathogen incidents that could extend this map's coverage further. Flagged for a future research pass rather than mined now, since its underlying entries have not yet been individually verified against this tracker's sourcing standard.

Context, not individually verified for mapping: Manheim & Lewis (2022) catalogued 71 high-risk human-caused pathogen exposure events from 1975–2016 — 65 accidental, 6 deliberate — with deliberate events roughly an order of magnitude more severe per event on average. That aggregate pattern is consistent with this map's own mix, though the two datasets were not cross-matched entry-by-entry.
